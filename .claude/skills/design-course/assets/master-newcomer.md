# Master Story — The Newcomer

A stranger arrives at a community and, over the course of the story, becomes
someone who can welcome the next stranger.

**This is the story spine, not the whole curriculum.** Each Movement holds about
48 chapters, of which roughly **5 are story chapters** — the beats below. The
other 43 are vocabulary sets, structures, practice, culture and checkpoints, built
around the spine (see `../SKILL.md` §"What fills a Movement").

Each Movement's story chapters run consecutively and are grouped under one scene,
so they read as a run. The arc across all ten Movements is registered as a
standalone `story_arc` with the four roles in `story_arc_cast`.

The beats below are one per Movement — the anchor. Expand each into its
Movement's five story chapters at generation time, keeping the same cast, the
same order, and the dialogue-first rule.

**This is a skeleton, not a script.** The beats carry the plot and the language
work; the names, foods, places, and customs are filled per language.

## The rule the story is built on: dialogue first

Movements 1–4 are **dialogue** (`style: skit`, `speaker` filled on every line).
Narration enters at Movement 5, when there is finally something to recount. The
oratory Movements are told aloud (`host_narrated`).

This is not a stylistic preference. Teaching traditions in these languages
overwhelmingly start with call-and-response — a greeting exchange, a question and
its answer — and a narrative opening defers the learner's first question form by
several Movements when the tradition puts it in lesson one. **Check the language's
own materials if any exist; if they teach through dialogue, this default is
right. If they genuinely don't, say so and adapt.**

## Cast

Four recurring roles. **Substitute names natural to the language and region —
never invent one that merely sounds plausible, and never use a role word as a
name.** Where a culture folds role and address into a single term, use it the way
speakers actually use it.

| Role | Who they are |
|---|---|
| **Newcomer** | the learner's counterpart; arrives knowing nothing |
| **Host** | takes the Newcomer in; the everyday voice |
| **Elder** | authority, ceremony, the formal register |
| **Child** | the Newcomer's peer in learning; asks the obvious questions |

Keep the same four names across all ten Movements so learners track them.

## 1 — Arrival · `skit` · path → compound

The Newcomer reaches the edge of the community and is greeted.

*Language: greeting exchange, names, where you are from, yes/no, thank you.*

1. **Host:** Welcome! You have come.
2. **Newcomer:** I have come. Good morning.
3. **Host:** What is your name?
4. **Newcomer:** My name is [Newcomer].
5. **Host:** Where are you from?
6. **Newcomer:** I am from far away.
7. **Host:** Are you tired?
8. **Newcomer:** Yes. I am tired.
9. **Host:** Come. Enter. This is my house.
10. **Newcomer:** Thank you.

**Localize:** the greeting formula and its response — most languages have a fixed
pair, and it belongs here verbatim. Whether one asks a stranger's name this
directly.

## 2 — The Household · `skit` · compound → hearth

The Newcomer is shown the compound, meets the family, and eats.

*Language: family words, possession, this/that, food, simple location, negation.*

1. **Host:** This is my wife. This is my child.
2. **Child:** Good morning.
3. **Newcomer:** Good morning. What is your name?
4. **Child:** My name is [Child]. Is that your bag?
5. **Newcomer:** Yes, it is my bag.
6. **Host:** Are you hungry?
7. **Newcomer:** Yes.
8. **Host:** There is food. There is no meat today.
9. **Child:** The food is good!
10. **Newcomer:** It is very good. Thank you.

**Localize:** the staple dish. Who eats first, and whether a guest is served
separately. The word for the compound.

## 3 — The Village · `skit` · path → market

The Host walks the Newcomer through the community.

*Language: places, occupations, numbers and price, greeting by role, asking directions.*

1. **Host:** Come, we are going to the market.
2. **Newcomer:** Is it far?
3. **Host:** It is not far.
4. **Host:** This woman sells fish. She is a trader.
5. **Newcomer:** Good morning. How much is the fish?
6. **Trader:** Two [units].
7. **Newcomer:** I will take two.
8. **Child:** That man is a teacher. He teaches at the school.
9. **Newcomer:** Where is the water?
10. **Host:** It is behind the market. We will go tomorrow.

**Localize:** currency and how amounts are said — many languages count in units
that are not decimal. What is actually sold. The market's name and its day.

## 4 — Growing Up · `skit` + short narration · water_land

The Newcomer goes with the Child to the water or the farm, and hears a proverb
for the first time.

*Language: past narration, sequence, quantity, first proverb.*

1. **Child:** We went to the water yesterday. We caught three fish.
2. **Newcomer:** Three? That is many.
3. **Child:** Today we will go again. Come with us.
4. *(narration)* They walked to the water in the morning.
5. **Child:** Look — hold it like this. Not like that.
6. **Newcomer:** It is difficult.
7. **Child:** It was difficult for me too. Then it was easy.
8. **Elder:** *[proverb about patience or learning]*
9. **Newcomer:** What does it mean?
10. **Elder:** It means the one who waits, eats.

**Localize:** the proverb — **do not invent one.** Use a real proverb the
community actually says, with attribution, or leave it as a placeholder for a
speaker. The livelihood: fishing, herding, farming, trading.

## 5 — The Threshold · `skit` + narration · sacred_grove → festival_ground

A coming-of-age happens in the community. The Newcomer witnesses it and is given
a small responsibility.

*Language: obligation and permission, formal address to an elder, request forms.*

1. *(narration)* On that day the whole community gathered.
2. **Host:** Today [Child] becomes an adult. You must not speak during the ceremony.
3. **Newcomer:** May I watch?
4. **Host:** You may watch. Stand here.
5. *(narration)* The Elder spoke, and the young people answered together.
6. **Elder:** *[formal charge — call]*
7. **All:** *[response]*
8. **Elder:** You — the one who came to us. Carry this.
9. **Newcomer:** I will carry it.
10. *(narration)* And so the stranger was given something to hold.

**Localize:** the ceremony itself is **heritage content** — never fabricated.
Leave the call, the response, and the rite as placeholders unless a keeper
supplies them. Everything around them can be written freely.

## 6 — The Working Year · `immersive_story` · water_land → market

A full season passes. The work is good, then it is not.

*Language: seasons and time, conditionals, cause and effect, comparison.*

1. *(narration)* When the rains came, the water rose.
2. **Host:** If the rain is heavy this year, the fish will be many.
3. *(narration)* For three months the work was good.
4. **Host:** We sold everything at the market. We bought cloth.
5. *(narration)* Then the rain stopped early, and the water fell.
6. **Newcomer:** What will we do?
7. **Host:** We will wait. It was worse two years ago.
8. **Child:** Were you afraid?
9. **Host:** I was afraid then. I am not afraid now.
10. *(narration)* The Newcomer learned that the year turns whether you are ready or not.

**Localize:** the seasonal calendar — rainy/dry, planting/harvest, high water/low
water. What is bought when there is money.

## 7 — The Union · `immersive_story` · compound → compound → festival

Two families negotiate a marriage. The Newcomer is inside the family now, and is
sent with the party.

*Language: negotiation, politeness registers, reported speech, indirect request.*

1. *(narration)* The Host's brother wished to marry, and the families met.
2. **Elder:** We have come to ask for your daughter.
3. **Bride's father:** You are welcome. Sit.
4. *(narration)* They spoke for a long time without saying what they meant.
5. **Elder:** They say the girl is hardworking.
6. **Bride's father:** She said she knows your family.
7. **Newcomer:** *(quietly)* Why does nobody say it directly?
8. **Host:** *(quietly)* Because saying it directly would end it.
9. *(narration)* At last the two families agreed, and there was a celebration.
10. **Elder:** Now you have two families. That is more than you had this morning.

**Localize:** the actual negotiation custom, the order of speakers, what is
exchanged. Indirection is near-universal here but its shape is not.

## 8 — The Assembly · `immersive_story` · assembly

A dispute comes before the elders. The Newcomer is asked what they saw.

*Language: argument and evidence, hedging, evaluation, disagreement politely.*

1. *(narration)* Two men disagreed about a boundary, and the matter came to the assembly.
2. **First man:** The land is mine. My father farmed it.
3. **Second man:** Your father farmed it. You did not.
4. **Elder:** You — you were there. What did you see?
5. **Newcomer:** I saw them both working. I do not know who is right.
6. **Elder:** That is an honest answer.
7. *(narration)* The elders spoke among themselves for a long time.
8. **Elder:** The land will be divided. Neither of you will be satisfied.
9. **Host:** *(afterwards)* That is how you know it was fair.
10. *(narration)* The Newcomer thought about that for a long time.

**Localize:** how disputes are actually settled and who has standing to speak.
Whether a newcomer would be asked at all — if not, change who is asked.

## 9 — The Elder's Voice · `host_narrated` · festival_ground

The Newcomer is asked to speak publicly for the first time, and uses a proverb
correctly.

*Language: oratory, praise, proverb deployed in context, formal opening and closing.*

1. *(narration)* At the festival the Elder said: the one who came to us will speak.
2. **Newcomer:** I am not from here. I do not speak well.
3. **Elder:** Speak anyway.
4. **Newcomer:** *[formal opening formula]*
5. **Newcomer:** When I came, I knew nothing. You fed me before you knew my name.
6. **Newcomer:** *[proverb about hospitality or belonging]*
7. *(narration)* The people answered, because that is what you do when a proverb lands.
8. **Elder:** Who taught you that?
9. **Newcomer:** [Child] taught me.
10. *(narration)* And the Elder laughed, and it was the best thing that had happened.

**Localize:** the opening and closing formulas of public speech, and the proverb.
Both are **real material or placeholders** — never invented. The audience
response is often fixed and belongs here verbatim.

## 10 — The Keeper · `host_narrated` · sacred_grove → compound → path

The Newcomer gives a blessing, and a new stranger arrives at the edge of the
community.

*Language: blessing, ancestry, the full formal register, the whole arc's vocabulary.*

1. *(narration)* Years passed. The Child had children.
2. **Host:** *(old now)* You have been here longer than you were anywhere else.
3. **Newcomer:** I have.
4. *(narration)* At the naming of the Child's first son, the Newcomer was asked to bless him.
5. **Newcomer:** *[blessing formula]*
6. **All:** *[response]*
7. *(narration)* Afterwards, walking back along the path, they met a stranger.
8. **Stranger:** Good morning. I have come from far away.
9. **Newcomer:** Welcome! You have come. What is your name?
10. *(narration)* And it began again.

**Localize:** the blessing is **heritage content** — a keeper supplies it or it
stays a placeholder. Movement 10's closing line should reuse Movement 1's opening line exactly;
the loop closing is the point.

## Generating story chapters

Each story chapter becomes one lesson CSV: metadata block, `---`, then the transcript
grid with `translation` carrying the English beat and `text` blank for a speaker.

```csv
title,The Newcomer — Arrival
description,A stranger reaches the community and is welcomed
style,skit
canDo,Greet someone and say where you are from
---
text,translation,speaker,roman
,Welcome! You have come.,Host,
,I have come. Good morning.,Newcomer,
```

Beats marked *(narration)* take an empty `speaker` — or the narrator name the
language's other lessons use.

Ten Movements is the full arc. A shorter cut that still works: **1, 2, 3, 6** —
arrival through a working year, which covers everyday life without needing
ceremony or heritage material.
